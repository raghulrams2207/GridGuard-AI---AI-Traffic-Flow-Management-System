About the project

Congestion spreads. So should the warning.

A jam at one junction rarely stays there. When a road ahead cannot take more cars, the queue backs up into the junction behind it and then the next. GRIDGUARD AI measures the queue on camera, estimates how far the trouble could travel, and compares fixes under the same conditions before recommending one. It then checks whether the fix worked.

How it works

01

Discover

Find a public traffic camera on ARGOS ATLAS and note its source and access rules.

02

Detect

OpenCV and a pretrained YOLO nano model draw boxes on cars, bikes, buses and trucks.

03

Measure

Count vehicles in a queue region, estimate occupancy and track how fast the queue grows.

04

Predict

A transparent risk score and a junction graph show where a bottleneck could spill back.

05

Simulate

SUMO with TraCI tests green-time changes, downstream protection and rerouting.

06

Verify

Re-measure after an action. If the queue did not improve, reject it and try the next best.

    About ARGOS ATLAS

    ARGOS ATLAS is a map that gathers traffic cameras and other context layers that are already public on official portals, and shows where each one comes from. Think of it as a phone book for public traffic cameras.

    In GRIDGUARD it has one job: helping you find a camera over a road or junction worth studying. Flights, ships and other layers are not needed.

    How to use it with GRIDGUARD

    1. Open the map and zoom to a road or junction with steady traffic.
    2. Click a camera. Note its name, location and the source it credits.
    3. Check whether it is live video or a still image that refreshes every few seconds or minutes.
    4. Read the source's licence and reuse terms.
    5. If the source allows it and gives a direct stream address, paste that address into Stream URL on the Live detection tab.
    6. If not, use a traffic clip you are permitted to use, upload it, and credit the ARGOS camera as the discovery source.

    Ground rules

    • Being on the map does not mean a stream your program can read. Always check.
    • Do not scrape pages or bypass access controls. A browser player address is not a public API.
    • Still images are snapshots only. Use video for queue growth.
    • No face or number-plate recognition.
    • One camera shows one spot. Use SUMO for connected-road tests and label which data is which.
    • Analysing a camera here never controls the real junction.

    FAQ

    Does GRIDGUARD control real traffic lights?

    No. It measures, scores and recommends. Fixes are tested only in simulation.

    Is the risk score a probability?

    No. It is a transparent heuristic: R = 0.35Q + 0.25O + 0.20G + 0.20D, tuned against simulation. Low is below 0.40, medium is 0.40 to 0.69 and high is 0.70 or more.

    How does the ambulance alert work?

    With tracking on, GRIDGUARD watches for two moving vehicles that overlap and stop, or a vehicle that stalls while traffic keeps flowing. It then sends a text to the number you set. For the demo that is a teammate. It is a heuristic, so expect false alarms, and it never contacts real emergency services.

    Do I need a GPU?

    No. It runs YOLO nano on the CPU. Use a smaller image size and a short clip if it feels slow.

    Where do the simulation numbers come from?

    From SUMO runs on a generated 6-junction grid with synthetic demand. They show how the controllers compare there, not how a real city behaves.

    Can I use my own video or camera?

    Yes. Upload a clip, pick a connected camera, open a file path, or paste a stream address you are allowed to use.
